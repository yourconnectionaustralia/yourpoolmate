Cloudy pool water: causes and fixes

Cloudy water is almost always one of five things. Test before you buy anything, because the fix for each is different.

By the Your Pool Mate teamUpdated 23 November 2026

  2. What to do, in order

The short answer

Cloudy pool water usually comes from high pH, not enough filtration, low or used-up chlorine, high calcium or alkalinity, or fine particles the filter can't catch. Test first: fix pH and chlorine, then run and clean the filter. Use a clarifier or flocculant only once the water is balanced.

  • Test pH, chlorine, alkalinity and calcium first
  • High pH is the most common culprit
  • Run the filter longer and clean it
  • Shock if combined chlorine is 0.5 ppm or more
  • Clarifier for haze, floc for a milky pool you can vacuum to waste

The five common causes

What to do, in order

  1. Test free chlorine, total chlorine, pH, alkalinity and calcium.
  2. Correct alkalinity, then pH, as in pH and alkalinity.
  3. Bring free chlorine into range, or shock if combined chlorine is 0.5 ppm or more.
  4. Run the pump for longer (at least one full turnover a day) and clean the filter.
  5. Give it 24–48 hours. Only then reach for a clarifier.

Cloudy after shocking?

Normal for a day or so. Shock can briefly raise pH and leaves dead organic matter for the filter to catch. Keep the pump running and clean the filter. If it's still cloudy after two days, re-test pH and calcium. Calcium hypochlorite (granular) shock adds calcium, which can tip a pool with already-high calcium into haze.

Cloudy salt pools

Salt chlorinators push pH up over time, so high pH is the usual suspect. Check pH weekly and keep acid on hand. A scaled-up cell also makes less chlorine, so check the plates too.

Common questions

Is it safe to swim in a cloudy pool?

Not if you can't see the floor or the main drain clearly. Cloudy water hides people in trouble, and it often means the chlorine isn't working well.

Clarifier or flocculant: which one?

Clarifier clumps fine particles so the filter can catch them, and works over a day or two. Flocculant drops everything to the floor overnight to be vacuumed to waste. Floc suits a very milky pool and a filter with a waste setting; cartridge filters usually suit clarifier.

How long should I run my pool pump?

Long enough to filter the whole pool at least once a day. For many home pools that's 6–8 hours in summer and 4 in winter, and longer while clearing a problem. Your pump's flow rate and pool volume decide the exact time.
