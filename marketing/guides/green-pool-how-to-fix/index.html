Green pool: how to fix it, step by step

A green pool is algae, and algae means the chlorine ran out. Here's how to clear it in a few days without buying half the shop.

The short answer

To fix a green pool: test the water, bring pH to 7.2–7.6, then shock with chlorine to around 10 ppm or more (higher if stabiliser is high), brush every surface, and run the filter around the clock. Hold the chlorine up and backwash or clean the filter as it clogs. Most pools clear in two to five days.

  • Cause: free chlorine dropped below about 1 ppm
  • Fix pH first so the chlorine works
  • Shock hard, then keep it up until the water is clear
  • Brush daily, run the pump 24 hours
  • Clean the filter often while it clears

Why pools go green

Algae spores are always blowing into your pool. They only take hold when there isn't enough free chlorine to kill them. That usually happens after:

  • a run of hot, sunny days that burns chlorine off faster than it's added,
  • heavy rain that dilutes the pool,
  • a salt chlorinator that's off, set too low, scaled up, or short of salt,
  • a pump that's been running too few hours, or
  • stabiliser that's too low (chlorine burns off in sun) or very high (chlorine is held back so hard it can't keep up).

Step by step

  1. Test everything. Free chlorine, pH, alkalinity and stabiliser. If the water is very green, a pool shop test is worth it.
  2. Clean out debris. Scoop leaves and empty the skimmer and pump baskets. Debris eats chlorine.
  3. Fix pH. Bring pH to 7.2–7.6. Chlorine is far weaker at pH 8.
  4. Shock with chlorine. Add enough liquid chlorine to lift free chlorine to around 10 ppm, or more if stabiliser is high. Pour it in front of a return jet with the pump running, in the evening so the sun doesn't eat it.
  5. Brush the walls, floor and steps. Algae hides in a slimy layer that chlorine can't reach until you break it up. Brush every day until it's clear.
  6. Run the pump 24 hours a day until the water is clear.
  7. Clean the filter. Dead algae clogs it fast. Backwash a sand or glass filter, or hose out cartridges, whenever pressure climbs.
  8. Test every morning and top up chlorine. Keep it high until the water is clear and chlorine holds overnight.
  9. Vacuum the dead algae once it settles as grey-brown dust. Vacuum to waste if your filter allows.

Don't swim until free chlorine is back below 5 ppm and the water is clear enough to see the main drain. Always follow the product label and add chemicals one at a time, never mixed.

How much chlorine does a shock take?

Liquid pool chlorine (about 12.5%) raises free chlorine by roughly 1 ppm for every 8 mL per 1,000 litres. So lifting a 50,000 L pool by 10 ppm takes about 4 litres. Our free chlorine dose calculator does the maths for your pool. Don't know your litres? Use the pool volume calculator.

The higher your stabiliser, the higher the shock needs to be. If stabiliser is above about 80 ppm, the fastest fix is often to drain and refill part of the pool to bring it down.

It's gone from green to cloudy. Now what?

That's progress: the algae is dead and the filter is catching up. Keep the pump running, keep cleaning the filter, and keep chlorine up. A clarifier can help a sand filter catch fine particles. See cloudy pool water.

Stop it coming back

  • Test at least weekly in summer, and keep free chlorine in 1.0–3.0 ppm (around 3 ppm in an uncovered outdoor pool).
  • Keep stabiliser at 30–50 ppm.
  • Run the pump long enough to turn the pool over at least once a day, more in summer.
  • Clean your salt cell when it scales, and keep salt where the manual says.
  • Brush weekly, especially corners, steps and behind ladders.

Common questions

How long does it take to clear a green pool?

Usually two to five days with a strong shock, daily brushing and the filter running around the clock. A very dark green or black pool, or one with high stabiliser, can take a week or more.

Should I use algaecide on a green pool?

Chlorine does the killing. Algaecide is a helper for prevention or stubborn mustard algae, not a replacement for shocking. Some algaecides foam, and copper-based ones can stain.

Why is my pool green but the chlorine reads fine?

Check pH and stabiliser. At high pH, or with very high stabiliser, chlorine can read in range but work poorly. Also check the strip isn't out of date, and that it's free chlorine you're reading, not total.

Can I swim in a green pool?

No. Green water means the chlorine isn't controlling germs, and cloudy water hides the floor, which is a drowning risk. Wait until it's clear and chlorine is back in range.
