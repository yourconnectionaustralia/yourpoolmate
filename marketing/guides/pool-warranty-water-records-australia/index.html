Pool warranties and your water records

Chlorinators, pumps, heaters and pool surfaces can all be damaged by badly balanced water. When a claim comes, a dated test history is your evidence.

The short answer

Many pool equipment and surface warranties exclude damage caused by incorrect water chemistry, such as low pH corroding a heater or high calcium scaling a salt cell. The best protection is a dated record of every water test and every chemical you added, kept by you rather than only by your pool shop.

  • Test at least weekly in the swimming season
  • Record the date, every reading, and what you added
  • Keep pool shop printouts, or photos of them
  • Keep receipts and the equipment's install date
  • Store it somewhere you can export it when asked

Why water chemistry matters to a warranty

Water that's out of balance damages pool gear in predictable ways, and manufacturers know it. Common examples:

  • Salt chlorinator cells: high pH and calcium coat the plates in scale; running with low salt wears the coating.
  • Heaters and heat pumps: low pH and low alkalinity corrode copper and titanium heat exchangers.
  • Pool surfaces: low calcium or low pH etches pebble, concrete and grout; high calcium and pH leave scale and stains.
  • Vinyl liners and covers: very high chlorine fades and weakens them.

Because of this, many warranty documents exclude damage from "incorrect water balance", "improper water chemistry" or "failure to maintain". If something fails and the maker suspects water, the question becomes: can you show how the water was kept?

Every warranty is different. Read your own documents for the words water balance, water chemistry and maintenance, and keep them with your pool records. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

Builder and statutory warranties

Pool construction in Australia is covered by state building laws, with statutory warranty periods for defects that vary by state and by the type of defect. A water record won't extend those periods, but it does answer the first question a builder may ask about a surface or structural issue: was the water looked after? Check the details with your state's building or fair trading regulator.

How often to test

Weekly in spring and summer and fortnightly in winter is a sensible baseline for most home pools. Test more often after heavy rain, a pool party or a heatwave. A steady weekly rhythm matters more than the occasional perfect test.

Keeping the record without a filing cabinet

Paper logbooks work if you keep them up. Most people don't. The practical options:

  • A spreadsheet: free, but easy to forget and easy to lose.
  • Your pool shop's system: better than nothing, but it's their record, and it only has the tests done in store.
  • An app that keeps it for you: Your Pool Mate saves every test and dose with its date, shows when a reading was corrected, and exports a PDF or a spreadsheet you can hand to a manufacturer, a builder or a buyer.

Selling the house

A pool with a documented history is easier to sell with confidence. Buyers and inspectors ask about the pool's condition. A tidy record of regular testing answers that before it's asked.

Common questions

Does my pool shop keep my water test records?

Usually only on their own system, and only for tests done in store. Ask for a copy of each printout, or photograph it, so you have your own record.

Will a water record guarantee my warranty claim is approved?

No. It's evidence, not a guarantee. The maker still assesses each claim against its own terms. What a record does is remove the easiest reason to say no.

Do I need to record winter tests too?

Yes, if you can. Fortnightly tests through winter show the pool was looked after all year, and winter is when many pools are quietly neglected.
