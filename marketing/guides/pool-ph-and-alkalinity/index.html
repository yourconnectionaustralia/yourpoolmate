Pool pH and alkalinity: which to fix first

pH decides how well your chlorine works. Alkalinity decides whether pH stays put. Get them in the right order and both get easier.

By the Your Pool Mate teamUpdated 23 November 2026

The short answer

Keep pH at 7.2–7.6 and total alkalinity at 80–120 ppm. Fix alkalinity first, because it holds pH steady. Raise alkalinity with sodium bicarbonate (buffer). Lower pH, and alkalinity with it, using hydrochloric acid or dry acid. Raise pH with soda ash. Re-test a few hours after each dose.

  • pH 7.2–7.6
  • Total alkalinity 80–120 ppm
  • Fix alkalinity before pH
  • Acid lowers both pH and alkalinity
  • Buffer raises alkalinity; soda ash raises pH

Why pH matters

At pH 7.5, roughly half your free chlorine is in its strong, germ-killing form. At pH 8.0 it's much less, so the same chlorine reading does far less work. That's why a pool can read "fine" for chlorine and still go cloudy or green.

  • pH too low (under 7.2): stings eyes, etches surfaces, corrodes metal and heaters.
  • pH too high (over 7.6): weak chlorine, cloudy water, scale on tiles and salt cells.

What alkalinity does

Total alkalinity is a buffer. With alkalinity at 80–120 ppm, rain, chlorine and swimmers barely move pH. Too low, and pH swings with every top-up. Too high, and pH keeps creeping up and is hard to bring down.

How to raise or lower each

ProblemProductNote
Alkalinity lowSodium bicarbonate (buffer)Raises alkalinity with only a small lift in pH.
Alkalinity highHydrochloric acid or dry acidLowers pH and alkalinity together; aerate to bring pH back up.
pH highHydrochloric acid or dry acid (sodium bisulphate)Pour slowly in front of a return jet, pump on.
pH lowSoda ash (sodium carbonate)If alkalinity is low too, buffer first.

Hydrochloric acid is dangerous. Wear gloves and eye protection, always add acid to water (never water to acid), never mix it with chlorine, and follow the label.

Salt pools and rising pH

Salt chlorinators push pH up as a side effect of making chlorine. A weekly acid top-up is normal for most salt pools. Keeping alkalinity toward the lower end (80–100 ppm) slows the climb.

The right order

  1. Test pH and alkalinity.
  2. Correct alkalinity into 80–120 ppm.
  3. Wait a few hours, pump running, and re-test pH.
  4. Correct pH into 7.2–7.6.
  5. Then adjust chlorine.

Big corrections are best done in two or three smaller doses with a re-test between, rather than one large dose.

Common questions

Why does my pool pH keep going up?

Salt chlorinators, fresh plaster or pebble, high alkalinity and lots of splashing or water features all push pH up. Keep alkalinity toward 80–100 ppm and expect to add acid regularly.

Can I swim after adding acid?

Wait at least four hours with the pump running, then re-test pH. Follow the label.

Will baking soda raise pH?

Sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) mainly raises alkalinity, with a small lift in pH. To raise pH by more, use soda ash.
